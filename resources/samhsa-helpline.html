SAMHSA National Helpline — One Free Call to Real Help

If you don’t know where to start, start here. One free phone call connects you to real treatment near you — day or night, even at 3 a.m., even if you don’t know what to ask for.

Start now

Call 1-800-662-HELP (4357)TTY 1-800-487-4889samhsa.gov

How to start — step by step

  1. Call 1-800-662-HELP (4357) — any time, day or night. (TTY: 1-800-487-4889.)
  2. Tell the specialist, in your own words, what is going on. There is no wrong way to say it — “I need help and I don’t know where to go” is a complete sentence here.
  3. They will search for treatment facilities, support groups, and programs near you that fit your situation — including options for people without insurance.
  4. Write down the names and numbers they give you. Ask them to repeat anything. Ask what each place costs — many have free or reduced-cost care.
  5. Prefer not to talk? Text your ZIP code to 435748 (HELP4U) to find help by text, or search yourself at FindTreatment.gov.
  6. Make the next call while you have the list in front of you — today, while the door is open in your heart.

What it is

SAMHSA is the U.S. Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration. Its National Helpline is a free, confidential treatment referral and information service for people and families facing alcohol, drug, or mental-health struggles. Trained information specialists listen and connect you to local treatment facilities, support groups, and community-based organizations.

Who it is for

Anyone — for yourself, or for someone you love. You do not need insurance, money, a diagnosis, or the “right words.” If it is on your heart enough to look at this page, it is enough to call.

What it costs

Free. The call is free and confidential, in English and Spanish, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. It is a referral service — calling never signs you up for anything and never obligates you to anything.

What to expect at the first step

A calm, unhurried conversation with a trained specialist — not a sales pitch, and not counseling (the helpline gives referrals and information, and says so plainly). You can stay anonymous. Nobody will judge the shape of your story; they hear every shape of it, every day.

If this moment is heavier than a guide can hold — if you feel you might harm yourself — call or text 988 right now. The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is free, confidential, and answers 24 hours a day. About 988
