Salvation Army Adult Rehabilitation — Free Residential Recovery

Sometimes recovery needs a new address. The Salvation Army’s rehabilitation centers take people in — to live, eat, heal, and work — for about six months, and the price is nothing.

What it is

The Salvation Army’s Adult Rehabilitation Centers (ARCs) are faith-based residential programs for adults struggling with alcohol and substance abuse. The program combines housing and meals with counseling, spiritual care, life-skills and work therapy — funded by Salvation Army thrift stores and donations, which is why it can be offered free of charge. The Fort Lauderdale center describes its program as six months, free of charge, for adult men ages 21–66.

Who it is for

Adults who need to step completely out of their current environment to recover. Centers serve their local communities — the South Florida centers below serve men; other centers nationally serve men and, at some locations, women. Call the center nearest you and ask — asking costs nothing either.

What it costs

Free of charge. Housing, meals, and the program itself cost the participant nothing. There is no insurance requirement and no bill.

How to start — step by step

  1. Find your nearest center through the locations search at salvationarmyusa.org, or start with the South Florida centers below.
  2. Call the center and ask about intake. They will tell you honestly whether there is an opening and what the program involves.
  3. Complete the intake conversation or interview. If the center is a fit, you will be given a start date and told what to bring.
  4. Pack light — clothing and essentials. The center provides your housing, meals, and program materials.
  5. No opening right now? Ask about the waiting list, and call the SAMHSA Helpline for other free options near you in the meantime.

What to expect at the first step

A structured day in a community walking the same road: work therapy, counseling, classes, and worship, with staff whose whole job is helping people rebuild. Six months sounds long from the doorway; from inside, it is the length of a new foundation.
