Alcoholics Anonymous — Free Meetings in Every Town

For almost ninety years, the simplest door into recovery from alcohol has been a folding chair in a room full of people who understand. It is still free, and it is still in your town.

What it is

Alcoholics Anonymous is a worldwide fellowship of people who share their experience, strength, and hope to help each other recover from alcoholism. Local groups hold free meetings — in person, online, and by phone — built around the Twelve Steps.

Who it is for

Anyone with a desire to stop drinking. A.A. states it as its only requirement for membership: a desire to stop drinking. You do not have to call yourself anything, believe anything, or be sure about anything else.

What it costs

Free. There are no dues or fees for A.A. membership — ever. At many meetings a basket is passed for voluntary contributions to cover rent and coffee; giving is always optional and never expected of a newcomer.

How to start — step by step

  1. Go to aa.org and open A.A. Near You.
  2. Enter your city or ZIP code to find your local A.A. office and its meeting list.
  3. Pick a meeting. Meetings marked “Open” welcome anyone; any meeting welcomes you if you have a desire to stop drinking.
  4. Just show up, a few minutes early. There is no sign-up, no registration, and no paperwork — walk in and take a seat.
  5. On your phone instead: the free Meeting Guide app finds meetings near you. Online and phone meetings worldwide are listed by the Online Intergroup at aa-intergroup.org.

What to expect at the first step

First names only. You will not be asked to speak, pay, sign anything, or explain yourself — sitting and listening is a complete and honored way to attend. Someone will likely greet you and offer coffee. What is said in the room stays in the room.
