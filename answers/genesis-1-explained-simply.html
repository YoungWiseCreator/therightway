What does Genesis 1 mean, explained simply?

Genesis 1 says that before anything else, God was already there — and that He made heaven, earth and every living thing by speaking. The chapter moves in order: first God forms the realms (light, sky, dry land), then He fills them (lights, birds and fish, animals and humankind). Humankind is made in God's image and given the earth to rule and keep. At the end of the sixth day God sees everything He made, “and, behold, it was very good” (Genesis 1:31, KJV).

The six days, in order

Day 1 — light, separated from darkness. Day 2 — the firmament, dividing the waters. Day 3 — dry land, seas, and plants. Day 4 — sun, moon and stars, for signs and seasons. Day 5 — fish and birds. Day 6 — land animals, then humankind, male and female, in God's image. The seventh day — God's rest — opens Genesis 2.

“In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.” — Genesis 1:1 (KJV)
