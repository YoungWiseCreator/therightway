Genesis 3: summary and meaning

Genesis 3 is the fall. The serpent questions God's one command — “Yea, hath God said…?” (Genesis 3:1, KJV) — the woman and the man eat of the one forbidden tree, their eyes are opened, and they hide. God comes asking, not accusing first: “Where art thou?” (3:9). Judgments follow for the serpent, the woman and the man — and inside the serpent's judgment sits the chapter's turning point, the first promise: the seed of the woman “shall bruise thy head” (3:15).

The order of the chapter

Verses 1–6 — the serpent, more subtil than any beast, misquotes the command and denies its consequence; the woman sees the tree is good for food, pleasant and desired to make one wise, eats, and gives to her husband. Verses 7–13 — eyes opened, fig leaves, hiding among the trees; God's questions — “Where art thou?”, “Who told thee that thou wast naked?” — and the answers passing blame: the woman, the serpent. Verses 14–19 — the judgments: the serpent cursed and set at enmity with the woman's seed (verse 15); sorrow in childbearing; the ground cursed, thorns and thistles, bread by the sweat of the face, and the return to dust. Verses 20–24 — Adam names his wife Eve, “the mother of all living”; God makes coats of skins and clothes them; the man is sent out of the garden to till the ground, and cherubim with a flaming sword keep the way of the tree of life.

The meaning the chapter itself carries: the break came through listening to a voice that misquoted God — and God's first move after it was to come looking, asking, and clothing. Continue: Genesis 2 summary.
