Genesis 2: summary and meaning

Genesis 2 slows down and looks closely at the sixth day. God finishes His work and rests on the seventh day, blessing it. He forms man from the dust of the ground and breathes life into him, plants a garden in Eden and puts the man in it “to dress it and to keep it” (Genesis 2:15, KJV). Seeing that it is not good for the man to be alone, God builds the woman from the man's side — and the chapter ends with the two together, unashamed.

The order of the chapter

Verses 1–3 — the seventh day: God rests, blesses and sanctifies it. Verses 4–7 — no rain yet, a mist watering the ground; man formed of dust, given the breath of life, becoming a living soul. Verses 8–14 — the garden planted eastward in Eden, the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and the river parting into four heads. Verses 15–17 — the man placed in the garden with one command: of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, “thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die” (2:17). Verses 18–25 — the animals named, no helper found among them, the deep sleep, the rib, the woman — “bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh” (2:23) — and marriage stated in verse 24.
