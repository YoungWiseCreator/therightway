Why does Genesis 1:26 say “Let us make man in our image”?

The honest answer starts with what the next verse settles: whatever the plural “us” means, verse 27 says God alone did the creating, and humankind bears God's image — no one else's. The plural word for God in the Hebrew of Genesis 1 is Elohim, plural in form. Readers have long offered three explanations, and this page carries them without forcing one past what the text says.

“And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth. So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.” — Genesis 1:26–27 (KJV)

The three readings

1. God addressing His heavenly court. Scripture elsewhere pictures God enthroned among the angels (Job 1:6; 1 Kings 22:19–20). On this reading God announces His purpose before them — but He alone creates, and man is made in God's image, not the angels'.

2. A plural of majesty or fullness. The plural expresses God's dignity and the fullness of His power — a royal way of speaking, keeping the chapter's clear teaching that there is one God (Deuteronomy 6:4).

3. God deliberating within Himself. The change from “let there be” to “let us make” marks this act as deliberate and personal — humankind is not summoned from the earth like the animals in verse 24, but made directly, patterned on God Himself.

What “image” means here

Not a physical copy — the chapter never describes God's form. The likeness is shown by what follows it: humankind is blessed and given dominion — to rule under God, represent Him, and keep what He made. Both male and female carry the image (verse 27).
