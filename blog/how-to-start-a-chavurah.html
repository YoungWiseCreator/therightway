How to Start a Chavurah in Your Neighborhood

A chavurah is a small circle of friends who study, eat, and look after their street. Starting one takes a table and two friends.

A chavurah (plural: chavurot) is a small group — the word grows out of chaver, friend. It is the oldest shape of Hebrew learning: not a lecture hall, but a table. Here is the whole method, the way our community practices it.

One: find two or three friends. Not ten — two. A chavurah that starts small and real beats a big one that starts as an announcement. Two: pick a weekly time and one home, and protect both. Three: walk the journey together — read or watch the chapter the channel is on, from the Journey map, and talk about what it actually says.

Four: end every gathering with one act of help for someone outside the circle — a meal carried, a ride given, a family walked to the programs on our Get Help page. Study that never leaves the table is only half the tradition. That is the whole seed. Plant it this week.
