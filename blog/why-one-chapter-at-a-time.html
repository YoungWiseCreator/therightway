Why We Read the Bible One Chapter at a Time

The whole Bible is 1,189 chapters. Read in order, one at a time, it stops being a reference book and becomes a road.

Most people meet the Bible in fragments — a verse on a card, a story half-remembered from childhood, a passage quoted at them. Fragments are easy to bend. A chapter, read whole and in its place, is much harder to bend: it says what it says, in the order it says it, with the chapters before and after it standing as witnesses.

That is why The Right Way walks the Bible one chapter at a time, in order, with no chapter skipped, paired, or padded. Genesis does not make sense without Exodus behind it; the Psalms land differently when you have walked the history they grew out of. The road teaches what the fragments cannot.

It is also a fairer pace. Most chapters run six to twelve minutes as episodes, so anyone can walk one chapter. Start where the road starts: Genesis 1, on the Journey map.
