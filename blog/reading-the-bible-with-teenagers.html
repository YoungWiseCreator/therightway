A Parent’s Guide to Reading the Bible With Teenagers

Teenagers can smell a softened story from across the room. Here is how we hand them the real one.

The fastest way to lose a teenager is to hand them a version of the story with the edges sanded off. They know the world is raw; a book that pretends otherwise reads as a book that is lying. That is why this channel presents the stories as written — and why our advisory opens every episode, honestly, before the story starts.

Walk it with them. One chapter is six to twelve minutes — short enough for a school night, long enough for a real conversation. Ask the only question that never fails: “What surprised you?” Teenagers will forgive a story for being ancient. They will not forgive it for being boring, and read whole, in order, it is not boring.

Give them ownership: a reading streak on the Journey map, a chapter race with their chavurah, a Hebrew word a week. Our Youth page carries the challenges and events built for exactly this.
