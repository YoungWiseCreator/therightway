Hebrew Words That Change How a Verse Reads

Ruach is breath, wind, and spirit in one word. Chesed is a loyalty English splits into five words. A short field guide.

Translation is a series of choices, and every choice hides something. That is why our episodes keep the Hebrew in view. Three examples to carry with you:

Ruach (רוּחַ) — breath, wind, spirit. English forces a translator to pick one; Hebrew holds all three at once, so the Spirit moving over the waters in Genesis 1 is also a wind, and also a breath. Chesed (חֶסֶד) — lovingkindness, mercy, covenant loyalty: a love that is also a promise kept. English scatters it across five words and loses the thread.

Shalom (שָׁלוֹם) — usually translated “peace,” but it means wholeness: nothing missing, nothing broken. When you know that, every blessing of shalom in the text doubles in weight. Our Study pages open one word like this for every chapter on the road.
