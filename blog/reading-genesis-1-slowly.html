Reading Genesis 1 Slowly: What the First Chapter Actually Says

Seven days, thirty-one verses, and a pattern hiding in plain sight. A slow walk through the first chapter of the road.

Genesis 1 is the most argued-over chapter in the Bible, which is a shame, because read slowly it is mostly a poem about order. Light before lights. Seas gathered before anything swims in them. Everything made “according to its kind,” everything seen and called good, and over the whole work a rhythm: evening and morning, evening and morning.

Notice what the chapter does not do. It does not explain itself. It does not argue. It declares — and then, on the seventh day, it rests, and calls the resting holy. The first thing in the Bible ever called holy is not a place or an object. It is a day.

Our first episode depicts the chapter exactly as written — 803 words, about seven minutes — with the Hebrew underneath the English where it matters. Watch how the journey opens, and then read the chapter yourself, slowly. The road starts here.
